# Ancestry health report

Summarizes the health-relevant content of a consumer ancestry DNA result in
plain language, explains evidence quality, and prepares the user for a clinical
conversation.

## Step 1 — identify the source and scope

Confirm:
1. **Platform** — 23andMe, AncestryDNA, MyHeritage, LivingDNA, or other
2. **What the user is sharing** — raw `.txt` DNA file, a PDF health report, or a
   pasted summary from the platform's website
3. **Focus** — all health categories, or a specific area (carrier status, polygenic
   risk scores, wellness traits, pharmacogenomics)

If the user uploads a raw DNA file, do not attempt to re-call variants from the
raw allele data. Instead ask them to share the specific variants or report sections
they want explained.

## Step 2 — categorize the findings

Group reported findings into:

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Disease risk (polygenic) | Type 2 diabetes, atrial fibrillation, late-onset Alzheimer's |
| Carrier status (Mendelian) | BRCA1/2, cystic fibrosis, sickle cell |
| Pharmacogenomics | CYP2C19 metabolizer status, DPYD |
| Wellness traits | Lactose intolerance, caffeine metabolism, sleep tendency |
| Ancestry composition | Used for context, not medical guidance |

## Step 3 — explain each finding

For each health finding, write:
1. What the gene or variant is and what it normally does
2. What the platform's reported result means (e.g., "elevated risk", "carrier")
3. Evidence quality: consumer polygenic risk scores have **limited clinical utility**
   for most traits — state this plainly
4. The absolute risk or carrier frequency where known (relative risk statements
   alone are misleading)
5. Whether this finding warrants clinical follow-up

## Step 4 — produce the summary

Write a structured report:
1. Finding table: category, gene/trait, result, evidence quality, follow-up recommended?
2. Plain-language explanation for each finding (from Step 3)
3. A section on limitations of consumer DNA health reports
4. Three to five questions to bring to a primary care physician or genetic counselor

## Limitations to always include

- Consumer polygenic risk scores are calculated on population averages and may not apply equally across ethnicities.
- A "not detected" result does not mean zero risk — panels cover a subset of known variants.
- Carrier status means one copy; most carriers do not develop the condition themselves.
- Consumer reports are not a substitute for clinical genetic testing ordered by a physician.

## Rules

- **Never give a clinical diagnosis or recommend treatment.**
- **Do not overstate the risk** implied by a polygenic score. Always provide context (e.g., average population risk vs. reported risk).
- Treat all genetic and health data as highly sensitive. Do not reference it outside this task.
- If a reported result includes a pathogenic BRCA, Lynch syndrome, or other high-penetrance variant, note that this warrants urgent referral to a genetic counselor.
